# AI for Finance & Business Teams — 60-Minute Workshop

## Purpose

Help non-technical teams turn everyday AI use into safe, repeatable workflows with clear evidence and human accountability.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the session, participants can:

- identify tasks that are suitable for AI assistance;
- structure a prompt around a real business outcome;
- separate source facts, model inference, and missing information;
- place human review at the right point;
- evaluate whether a workflow is useful and safe.

## Session plan

| Time | Topic | Participant outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 min | AI beyond the chatbot | Understand the difference between a prompt and a business workflow |
| 10–20 min | Use-case selection | Choose a task with clear inputs, outputs, owner, and review point |
| 20–35 min | Prompt-to-workflow exercise | Build a constrained prompt using approved evidence |
| 35–45 min | Verification and risk | Test for unsupported claims, missing evidence, and confidential data |
| 45–55 min | Human-in-the-loop design | Assign review, correction, approval, and escalation responsibilities |
| 55–60 min | Action plan | Define one controlled experiment and success measure |

## Live exercise

Participants bring a recurring, text-heavy task such as summarising a meeting, comparing documents, drafting a status update, or extracting requirements.

They complete this template:

1. **Business task:** What must be accomplished?
2. **Approved inputs:** What evidence may the AI use?
3. **Constraints:** What must the AI avoid or leave unknown?
4. **Required output:** What format makes review easier?
5. **Reviewer:** Who checks facts and approves use?
6. **Success measure:** Time saved, errors reduced, or clarity improved?

## Demonstration prompt

> Use only the supplied material. Separate verified facts from missing information and inference. Produce: (1) evidence present, (2) contradictions, (3) information gaps, and (4) recommended next actions. Do not invent facts or make the final decision. Cite the source section for every material claim.

## Facilitator notes

- Start with participants' real work rather than AI terminology.
- Ask who carries responsibility if the output is wrong.
- Demonstrate one incorrect output and the verification method.
- Never ask participants to paste restricted customer, employee, credential, or commercially sensitive data into an unapproved tool.
- Treat a controlled draft workflow as a stronger first experiment than autonomous action.

## Evaluation rubric

Score each dimension from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong):

- usefulness;
- factual accuracy;
- evidence traceability;
- clarity;
- time saved;
- recovery when inputs are incomplete;
- quality of human review;
- privacy and control fit.

## Follow-up

Within one week, participants run the workflow on three controlled examples, record failures, refine the instructions, and decide whether to stop, continue testing, or integrate it into an approved process.
